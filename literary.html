Literary research

My literary research interests lie mostly in what books try to withhold: unreliable narrators, characters a story tries to erase, and the forms, like footnotes, redactions, and punctuation, that let them back in.

I’m also interested in postmodernism, metafiction, gender and sexuality, and magical realism.

Projects

English BA thesis
2026–27

Erasure and the archive in Justin Torres’s Blackouts

How the novel layers fiction and nonfiction, fragments its chronology, and uses redaction and paratext to carry what the main text suppresses. I draw on Foucault, Derrida’s hauntology, Sedgwick’s reparative reading, Heather Love, and Spivak.

Literary research and digital humanities
With Prof. Malynne Sternstein

Who is speaking in Lolita?

I’ve read Lolita through Foucault’s heterotopia and productive power, Lacan on the gaze, Derrida’s hauntology, and Sedgwick’s reparative reading, asking whose voice we hear at any moment, Nabokov’s, Humbert’s, or Dolores’s, and when Dolores’s voice can be recovered at all.

The computational side looks at typography: how brackets, parentheses, and punctuation work in the novel. Across the English and Russian texts, I track how the book uses certain punctuation, words, and metaphors, mirrors especially, and how they relate to voice and meaning.
